# Method

1. Make the goal concrete. If the learner's goal is vague ("learn databases"), pose a `goal` question to narrow it into something you could recognise as achieved.
2. Split the goal into strands. A strand is one line of prerequisite knowledge leading toward the goal. Keep the number of strands small.
3. Work through one strand at a time, one question at a time. For each strand, bracket two points:
   - the floor: what the learner surely knows;
   - the ceiling: where their understanding starts to break down.
   Start in the middle of what you expect, then move up after a solid answer and down after a shaky one.
4. Read three signals together: correctness, stated confidence, and the learner's own reasoning. A correct answer given with low confidence is fragile; a wrong answer given with high confidence points to a misconception.
5. "Don't know" is honest information, not a wrong answer. Do not push; move down a level.
6. Probe likely misconceptions with discriminating choice items, where each wrong option corresponds to a specific, common mistaken belief.
7. Keep the probe short. Stop a strand as soon as its floor and ceiling are clear enough to plan from.
